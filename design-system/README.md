Gav-IL is HackIllinois 2027's judging and admin platform. It wears the 2027 brand (HackIllinois 2027 Design System and Registration Figma files) pushed toward the **rodeo / old west** of the 2027 Theme Moodboard and Website files: wanted-poster paper, saloon wood, signposts, rubber stamps, sheriff stars and rodeo rosettes. Judges use it on their phones, walking between tables in a loud room, so it shows one task per screen, puts every piece of content on high-contrast poster paper, and always says where to go next (on a signpost). Staff and admins use the same tokens in a denser desktop console.

## Content fundamentals

- **Talk to the judge as "you", in short imperative sentences.** "Head to your room." "Pick the stronger project." Never "Please kindly proceed to…".
- **Buttons start with a verb and name the result:** "Submit evaluation", "Confirm: NeuralMesh", "Clear, send to judging". Never "OK", "Yes" or "Done".
- **Numbers people act on are written as they appear on signs:** "Room 4217", "Table A12" (a table code is the room letter plus the 2-digit table on the printed placard). Set them in `--type-numeral` or `--type-caps`, tabular.
- **Table codes are room-qualified and printed:** "A12" is Room 4217, table 12. Each placard also prints a 2-character check after a dot ("A12 · K7"); the app never shows the check before the judge reads it at the table.
- **Use the official terms exactly, and never mix them:** *Path* (Pro, General), *Track* (company-sponsored: Stripe, Modal…), *Prize* (HackIllinois, company, MLH). A judge "nominates" for prizes and "compares" projects; the app never says "rate" or "score" to a judge. "Score" is only an admissions word.
- **Anti-cheat copy states evidence, not guilt:** "3 commits 40 min after the 9:00 AM deadline", not "Cheated".
- **Use sentence case everywhere.** Uppercase appears only in `eyebrow` overlines. No emoji.

## Visual foundations

**Materials.** Four materials, each with one job. Never invent a fifth.
- **Ground** (`ground`): the sky behind everything. Style Guide purple at **Sunset**, Registration night blue at **Night**. On a judge screen it is **painted**: the 2027 canyon (`art-ground`, the sunset or the night painting) cover-fit under the content with the `scrim` token over it (see Brand art). Only titles (`display`), eyebrows (`gold-on-ground`, on a plum plaque) and short ledes (`on-ground-soft`) sit on it; the flat `ground` colour is the fallback while the painting loads.
- **Paper** (`surface`, `surface-sunken`, grain texture `--gv-grain`): every card, sheet, table and drawer is a wanted poster. A printed double rule (`line`) frames cards. It stays cream in both themes so text reads the same day or night.
- **Wood** (`wood`, `wood-deep`, `wood-hi`, texture `--gv-wood`): saloon planks for chrome only: the app bar, the timer band (`wood-deep`), the sticky action bar, the admin header and tabs. The signs you read and press are the real 2027 art, not a texture: the wood button and Back/Next (`art-plank-*`), the inline Wayfinding sign and the hero signpost (`art-signpost`). Lettering on wood is `on-wood` / `on-wood-soft`; on the signpost's light tan signs it is `ink-sign`.
- **Rope and brass** (`rope`, `brass`, `--gv-rope`): decoration only. Rope dividers, the sheet grab handle, the rosette ring and the sheriff star. Never text, never a control.

**Rodeo patterns** (each maps to a real job, not decoration):
- *Signpost* = wayfinding. The inline `Wayfinding` is the arrow plank (its tip points right: "go here"); the hero is the real blank signpost lettered with live Oswald text (the label on the top board, "ROOM" or "TABLE" and the number on the arrows, the floor plan on the second arrow).
- *Wanted poster* = the project you're judging. `ProjectHeader` centers the name in Ultra under a ruled eyebrow ("NOW JUDGING"), in `ink-soft`, never path-colored.
- *Ticket stub* = the order tag on compare cards ("Earlier", "Just now").
- *Rubber stamp* (`Badge` with `gv-badge--stamp`) = a status: JUDGED, SKIPPED, admissions status. An outline in the stamp color, never a fill.
- *Sheriff star* (`gv-star`) = done. Round 1 complete and Round 2 submitted. It stamps in once with a slight overshoot (`ease-stamp`), then the title, lede and sign follow it; under reduced motion it simply appears. On Round 1 complete it stands between a horseshoe and a cactus (`gv-win`), and the table progress (`TableProgress`: the Figma sheriff stars, one per table) is its row of filled stars.
- *Garland* = a break between two sections (`Garland`): cream horses on the painting, brown on paper. Never two in a row.
- *Prop* = a state with nothing to do: the wagon waits with the judge, a cactus is an empty list (`Prop`, `EmptyState prop`).
- *Rosette* = a top-3 place in Round 2 rankings.
- *Star* icon = nominate for a prize (it replaces the trophy). It is a clean 5-point star; the 6-point sheriff star is only the 96px badge.

**Color.** Body text on paper is `ink` (#3D2B54, the Style Guide's body text/outline color exactly; 12.6:1 on white, 11.5:1 on paper). Secondary text uses `ink-soft` and `ink-muted` (both pass 4.5:1 on `surface-sunken`).
- `gold` with `on-gold` is the primary action, on paper and on wood. Gold never means "careful": the offline strip uses `warn-tint` over paper with a `warn` edge.
- `select` (plum) with `select-tint` marks anything chosen: chips, compare cards, options.
- Paths: Pro is `pro`/`pro-tint` (terracotta) and General is `general`/`general-tint` (purple), always with the word.
- Status colors `warn`, `danger` and `success` always come with an icon or word. Tints (`*-tint`) are composited over paper inside badges and chips, so they read the same on any row tint.
- `terracotta`, the sky tokens, `rope`, `brass` and `gold-glow` (the cover sun's halo) are identity only: never small text.
- `rec` (the danger red, named for its job) marks recording: the record disc, its dot and the word **Recording**, never colour alone. `rec-ring` is its breathing halo. `viewfinder` / `on-viewfinder` are the camera frame of a placard scan.
- Placards are print, not a fifth material: `room-a` … `room-d` (Gold, Teal, Rose, Sage) band each room's placards, with `on-room-*` text.

**Type.** All Google Fonts, the Style Guide stack:
- **Ultra** (`--font-display`) for screen titles (with the `shadow-title` hard drop) and project names on posters and compare cards.
- **Oswald** (`--font-condensed`), uppercase and tracked, for buttons, eyebrows, labels and numbers in tables and timers.
- **Manrope** (`--font-sans`) for everything people read: descriptions, notes, rows. 16px minimum in fields so iOS never zooms.
- **Rye** (`--font-western`) is sign lettering: room and table numbers on signposts, the sign-in greeting, the poster eyebrow, rosette ranks and the "vs". Short strings only (never a sentence, never below 13px).

Every type style is a token: `font: var(--type-<name>); letter-spacing: var(--tracking-<name>)` (display-lg, display, display-sm, room-hero, count-hero, h2, stat, title, title-sm, timer, button-lg, button, caps, numeral, eyebrow, body, body-strong, body-sm, label, read-aloud for a script spoken at the table, and the print-only placard-code and placard-check). Never write a px font size in a component. Manrope has no italic: never set text italic. Every number uses tabular figures.

**Shape.** The Figma chamfer is the signature:
- Filled shapes (buttons, tags) cut their corners with `clip-path`: 8px on phones (Mobile Design System) and 16px on web (Web Design System dev note: 16w × 12h). Outlined controls on paper (chips, fields, options, selects, score) use a 4px radius instead, because a clip-path would cut their border at the corners.
- Back/Next buttons are arrow-shaped (`shape="back"|"next"`).
- Radii are tokens: `radius-sm` (4px, outlined controls), `radius-md` (6px, cards where bevel is unsupported), `radius-lg` (16px, sheet tops). Arrow points are `arrow-tip` (18px) with a `cut-md` notch; ticket-stub bites are `notch`.
- Lines are tokens too: `stroke-hair` (1px rules and frames), `stroke` (2px edges that must read), `stroke-focus` (3px focus and the strongest selection ring). Paired rings are sums of strokes; the card's double rule is `shadow-rule`, plank edges `shadow-plank-edge`/`-up`, the rosette ring `shadow-rosette`. The procedural textures are built from tokens too: wood grain and rope stops sit on the 4px grid a hairline either side, nail heads are `nail`. Without `color-mix()` (Safari before 16.2) the planks keep their shading and lose only the translucent grain lines.
- Cards bevel their corners with CSS `corner-shape: bevel` where the browser supports it (Chrome/Android). Elsewhere (iOS Safari today) they fall back to 6px corners, keeping the ledge.
- Pills only for badges.

**Touch.** Every judge-side control and link is at least `tap-min` (48px). Chips keep 8px gaps, so hit areas never touch. Controls set `touch-action: manipulation` and no OS tap flash; each has its own `:active`. Hover styles live only under `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`. A control that appears where the judge just tapped (the next project's Submit, Undo, Next table, Change my ranking, an inline confirm's buttons, Sign out after a sheet closes over it) ignores taps for `dur-settle`, so a double tap never acts on something unseen; actions that share a slot across screens get their own `settleKey`. A settling button absorbs the tap (it never falls through to what is underneath) and does not look pressed. Keyboard and assistive-tech presses always act (focus only reaches a new control when the app moves it there), but a held Enter or Space never auto-repeats into a button. Compare cards ignore taps for `dur-settle` after the pair appears, a second Confirm or Submit in the same frame is a no-op, and a Round 2 row moves once per double tap. In a list, a card that goes lets the next one slide up under the pointer: a `FlagCard` list passes `settleKey` (its ids and levels, in order), and both buttons of every card ignore pointer taps for `dur-settle` after the list changes. A sheet a finger closes (a button, the scrim, a drag) lays a clear shield over the page (`GavIL.tapShield()`) for `dur-exit` + `dur-settle`, so the second tap on **Close map** or **Back** never lands on Submit or Skip underneath; a button the finger pressed also keeps its own footprint shielded until `dur-retap` (1.5 s), because the consent sheet's **Everyone agreed · start** sits exactly over Submit and a judge who thinks nothing happened taps again after a second. Only that spot is held back; the rest of the page answers as soon as the sheet has gone. Escape and Enter never shield.

**Narrow phones.** 390px is the target; 360 and 320 still never scroll sideways. A bar's commit label wraps onto two lines inside the same 56px button when both labels can't share one line (a ghost's invisible side padding is trimmed first), and the offline tag swaps to a short line ("2 saved here") and drops its icon instead of cutting its message with an ellipsis.

**Buttons.** Gold fill = primary (one per screen, on the right of the bar). Inked edge on paper (a sunken plank on wood) = secondary. Text = ghost; danger text = `ghost-danger` (a trigger on paper). Solid `danger` = only the destructive commit inside a confirm. Disabled never fades: it becomes a readable sunken plate (wood-deep on wood). `loading` keeps the fill and shows a spinner with the label.

**Feedback.** Toasts float above the bar that triggered them and never move the page; the same words twice show again (a new `id`), each for `dur-toast`. Destructive actions confirm inline, on paper, next to what they act on (`InlineConfirm`): Cancel left, commit right. Android buzzes (`GavIL.haptic`) when judging begins, at 10 min, 2 min and time up, when a vote is recorded and on errors; iPhones get the visual cue (the timer band flashes once).

**Motion.** Motion is a token family (`tokens.json` → `motion`), so CSS and JS share it:
- Press in `dur-press` (80ms), release `dur-release` (160ms), scale `press-scale` (0.96; 0.98 for big surfaces).
- Enter on `ease-out` (`dur-enter` 220ms, `rise` 8px, `stagger` 40ms); leave faster on `ease-in` (`dur-exit` 120ms). Sheets slide up over `dur-sheet` (300ms) on `ease-drawer` and drag down to dismiss.
- Screen changes go through `GavIL.swap()`: a View Transition when available (only the screen body moves), else the CSS rise-in.
- Round 2 swaps animate with FLIP (`ease-in-out`). The timer fill and the undo fuse scale with `transform`, never `width`.
- Reduced motion is decided once, in tokens: every motion token may carry a `reduced` value, emitted under `prefers-reduced-motion` and `:root[data-motion="reduce"]`. `--motion` becomes 0, so slides become fades, presses and stamps stop moving, the chevron flips without turning, the undo fuse fades instead of draining, decorative loops (`loop`: urgent dot, skeleton) stop, and a dragged sheet no longer follows the finger. JS (`GavIL.motionOK()`) skips FLIP and View Transitions. Color feedback stays (the band's colour at a timer threshold, a disabled plate), but nothing moves or pulses: the busy spinner holds still (its iteration count is `loop`, 0 here) and the timer flash does not run, so busy is said in words and `aria-busy`, and a threshold crossing by the live region and the colour.
- Recording breathes, it doesn't flash: the halo and dot pulse on `dur-rec-pulse` (1.8 s, `ease-pulse`) and hold still under reduced motion; the level meter follows the mic on `dur-meter` and becomes words ("Hearing speech" / "Quiet") under reduced motion. A matched table check stamps in (`ease-stamp`). Product timings are tokens too, never reduced: `dur-cant-find` (3 min en route), `rec-warn` (7:00) and `rec-cap` (8:00). `demo-talk-rate` and `demo-wait` are prototype-only.
- JS timing reads the same tokens (`GavIL.tokenMs`): `dur-autosave` (notes sync pause), `dur-toast`, `dur-undo` (the vote window, a product rule, never reduced), `dur-settle`, `dur-retap`, and the sheet's flick physics (`flick-distance`, `flick-speed`). The prototype's fake network latency is `demo-latency`; components never read it.

**Focus.** Clipped shapes can't show outlines, so focus is a 3px inset ring: `focus` (ink) on paper, `on-wood` (cream) on wood, except a secondary on wood, which is a cream plank and takes the ink ring. A ghost (clear) button gets an ink band with a cream band inside it, so its ring reads on paper and on the ground (Sign out on My notes). **A wood plank** (the wood button, Back, Next) takes a cream band that follows the plank's own cut (the chamfer, or the 45-degree arrow) one `stroke-focus` in from its edge, so the plank keeps its dark edge outside the ring: a cream ring flush with the edge melts into a paper card (cream on paper is 1.04:1) and reads as the plank shrinking. The band is 7.8:1 on the grain inside it and the plank's own edge is over 10:1 on paper. It is a pseudo-element drawn as the difference of two inset copies of the plank's polygon; the arrows' corners move by the `plank-ring-*` tokens, which the suite recomputes from `plank-tip`. The suite measures the band against the plank and against what the plank sits on (paper, the ground, a wood bar), checks each edge of the band is a uniform inset of the cut, and fails a flush ring on paper. Every ring has a band at least `stroke` wide that differs ≥ 3:1 from what it covers, in both themes; the suite checks each control on each screen with `focus({ focusVisible: true })`. A focused control that is also selected adds a paper gap and a second ring, so focus never looks like selection. The tab the keyboard moved to carries `data-kb-focus`, so its ring shows even where `:focus-visible` guesses wrong.
- Focus is never dropped on the page. A screen change focuses the new screen's first heading (every judge screen has one; on evaluate it is the project name), or the body while it loads; one polite live region speaks what focus can't. Confirm moves focus to Undo and Undo back to Confirm (a tap that never focused the button included). A sheet makes everything behind it `inert`, starts focus on its own title unless it names a better first stop (the skip sheet's first reason), so no keyboard pops up over a field, and returns focus to its opener. An admin tab panel is a tab stop only when it opens with content (a heading, an alert), never when it opens with a control (APG). A tab panel and a scrolling region that are tab stops show a 3px `gold-on-ground` ring. A region that scrolls is reachable by keyboard: with a control inside, Tab reaches that; a screen of text has none, so the shell's `main` becomes `tabindex=0` with its heading as its name while it scrolls (the clickable frames pin the phone's height; the shipped app scrolls the document and never does), and stops being one when it does not. An inline confirm is not modal: focus starts on Cancel, Escape cancels, Tab moves on; it returns focus to its opener, or to the nearest panel when the row it acted on is gone. Any action that removes the control that ran it (a cleared flag, a bulk bar that closes, Save changes becoming a status line) hands focus on with `GavIL.keepFocus`.
- Pages name their language (`<html lang="en">`; `build_previews.py` adds it to a source that has none) and headings step down one level at a time: the project name is a screen's h1, a card under it is an h2 (`Card` and `TableTalk` default `as` to `h2`), a card inside an h2 section passes `as="h3"`. The admin console has one visually hidden h1, a `main` landmark, h2 sections and h3 cards; the suite walks the outline of the evaluate screen, the waiting screen and every admin tab.
- Nothing is announced every second: the timer speaks threshold crossings, the vote says "You can undo it for 8 seconds" once, and Undo's name never changes.

## Judge flow rules

1. Screen 1 (check-in confirm) shows the judge's path, Round 1 room and Round 2 prize. A judge who confirms has seen all three.
2. The countdown shows the room as a hero `Wayfinding`, and the first project appears without a refresh.
3. **A visit has two phases.** *En route* (`EnRoute`): the table on the hero signpost (its last board says "Look for" and the project; the team's first names are under it), the table progress plaque ("Next table · 1 of 4"), **Skip** and **I'm at the table**; after `dur-cant-find` (3 min) it offers **Can't find it?** (floor plan, ask staff). *At the table* (`TableCheck`, above the poster): "Are you NeuralMesh? Team: Ana, Dev, Kim", then **Scan table QR** or type the placard code ("A12 · K7"). Notes, nominations, Table talk, the demo status and **Submit** stay locked until it matches; **Skip** never locks. A mismatch names the project at that code and says where the right table is ("A14 is LedgerLite. NeuralMesh is at A12, two tables to your right."); a missing check asks for "the 2 characters after the dot" with no penalty, a misread character (O, 0, I, 1, L…) gets a hint and no penalty, and 5 wrong checks lock typing (scan and the team's name still work). A pasted placard URL counts as a scan only with the QR's signed token (`?k=`). Offline the phone still catches a wrong table (it has the room plan) and still asks for the check; the check (or the scan's token) is accepted for now and verified on reconnect: a check that doesn't match locks judging again at the current table ("doesn't match A12's placard… type the 2 characters again", notes kept), and a past table's is flagged for staff. A reload never strands a check: one in flight runs again, an open scan closes. **Confirmed by name · staff may check** (no placard) asks a second question first ("Did the team say “NeuralMesh” themselves? Only confirm if you gave no hint."); then it unlocks notes and Submit, holds nominations until staff check the table, and can't report the team absent or start Table talk. The phone never holds a check: what was typed or scanned goes to the server (`GavIL.mockServer` in the previews), which answers matched, wrong table, check needed, bad check or locked.
4. Evaluate: `TimerBar` at the top, then inline `Wayfinding` ("Table A12" on one line, the room in its label), the `TableCheck` strip, `ProjectHeader` (the project name is the screen's `h1`), `Disclosure` rows (closed; their links are 48px link buttons), `TableTalk`, `DemoStatus`, `NotesField` (autosaves) and `NominationPicker`. `ActionBar` holds ghost **Skip** (it asks for a reason) and primary **Submit evaluation**. While the project loads, `ProjectSkeleton` (`rows: 3`) holds the poster and its rows at their exact height; notes and nominations are there from the start, so nothing moves when the project arrives.
5. Every evaluation after the first leads to `ComparePair`: the question is the title, the names are on the cards, and both cards plus the confirm bar fit a 390 × 844 phone. The confirm button names the chosen project.
6. **My notes** is in the app bar on every in-judging screen. Each judged project shows its `RecallBlock` and opens an editor with its highlights, demo status, notes and nominations until Round 1 ends.
7. At zero the timer says "Time is up", Skip disappears, and Submit becomes **Submit final evaluation**. The judge finishes the current project and casts one last vote; its forward button says **Finish Round 1**, not Next table. **Round 1 complete** then shows the sheriff star between a horseshoe and a cactus, the finished star row, a garland and the deliberation room on the hero signpost (the lede says when Round 2 opens), and My notes turn read-only (and say so).
8. Skip opens `SkipSheet`. The reason decides what happens next: absent team → re-queued for another judge (two absent reports from different judges hold the table and give staff a task); team busy with another judge → no penalty, it comes back after the judge's next table (if it was the last free one, the judge waits); can't find table → staff pinged and the table moves to the end of the queue (if it is the last table, **Staff are on it** waits there: "I found it" or end Round 1 without it); conflict → never shown to this judge again. Until Round 1 ends, a skipped project in My notes can be put back; it comes up after the current table.
9. After confirming a vote, the bar shows **Vote recorded**, **Undo (n)** above and **Next table** where Confirm was; a gold fuse drains the 8-second window (`dur-undo`), then the vote commits. Next table commits at once. The deadline has one owner (the vote's timestamp), so leaving for My notes and coming back neither restarts nor double-commits it. Undo is checked against the wall clock: once the window is over (a late timer, a phone that slept, the frame before the next screen) it is refused and shows disabled, and the old screen keeps saying **Vote recorded** until the new one replaces it.
10. Offline: the compact `OfflineBanner` takes the wordmark's place in the app bar (a fixed-height paper tag), so going offline or back moves nothing on the screen. Judging continues; every note, nomination, submit, skip, staff ping, vote and the Round 2 ranking joins one queue on the phone (counted the moment it happens) and the queue sends on reconnect. A note typed online whose autosave fires after the network drops joins the queue too. Toasts never claim what can't have happened offline ("Staff get the ping when you reconnect", not "Staff pinged"). Drafts and the queue are saved on the device (the prototype uses localStorage; the web app should use IndexedDB), so a reload or a discarded tab loses nothing. Notes keep their draft on every keystroke; a pending autosave is flushed on submit, unmount and reconnect, and reconnecting re-sends notes that were only saved on the phone, so their status turns to **Saved**. Notes that fail to save say **Not saved · Retry** and keep the text. Signing out with an unsent queue says how many items would be deleted and asks for exactly that.
11. Round 2 is on the same phone and opens when nominations close, 15 minutes after Round 1 ends (the Round 1 complete lede says the time, "Round 2 opens at 8:30 PM, when nominations close", and the sign says why: nominations close 15 minutes after Round 1 ends). The judge ranks their prize's finalists with `DeliberationBoard` and submits. Before then the Start Round 2 button still opens the page, but the page is a closed state, never an empty board: the step ("Round 2 · Best UI/UX"), the hero signpost saying "Opens at 8:30 PM, when nominations close.", and a card, "Finalists aren't set yet. Nominations are still coming in. The finalists appear here when Round 2 opens." (the web build shows the same for a 409 `RoundNotOpen`). The prototype opens Round 2 at once, because a demo cannot wait 15 minutes; the demo strip's "Round 2 not open yet" shows the closed page and "Open Round 2" lets the clock pass.
12. **Waiting:** when every remaining table is with another judge, the judge sees "All your tables are busy. We'll buzz you when one frees up." The screen moves on by itself, with a `go` buzz and a toast naming the table. Never "Round 1 complete" while tables are owed. The covered wagon waits with the judge, and the star tracker says how many tables are done.
13. **Table talk** (judge-private, off per event until counsel signs off): **Ask and record** opens `ConsentSheet`, a script read aloud that names the processors (Cloudflare transcribes, Anthropic summarizes; the audio isn't kept; the notes are only for judging). The engine starts only on **Everyone agreed · start**, and its microphone request runs inside that very tap (iOS opens the microphone only from a gesture); **Not this time** records nothing and stores no reason, and Escape, the scrim or a drag only close the sheet (no decision, nothing is declined). The first choice wins, and a dismissal that has started counts as one: a yes tapped while the sheet is still leaving is ignored, so the microphone never opens for a sheet the judge was closing. After a yes the card says "Waiting for the microphone…" and nothing says Recording until the engine is listening. One recording and one retry per table. While recording, the word **Recording**, a red dot and the timer are always on screen, with **Pause**, a 72px **Stop**, **Mark this** and **Stop and delete** (nothing is pulled out or kept; the try stays used). There are no live captions: nothing on screen repeats what is said, and the phone does no speech recognition (the browser's own recognizers send audio to Google or Apple, which the consent script doesn't name). Pausing, leaving the screen (My notes), a locked phone or hidden page, Skip, signing out and the admin switch all stop every microphone track; Resume needs a tap and asks for a fresh stream. Stop pulls highlights on the phone (`GavIL.talk.extract`); each needs **Keep**, **Edit** or **Remove**, and **Keep** adds it to Private notes. Highlights describe, never judge (no score, rank or winner words); a prize suggestion is a card with its quote and needs a **Nominate** tap; Most Popular is never suggested. Recall rows on Compare and in My notes come only from what the judge kept or tapped (two lines at most). Suggestions the judge hasn't reviewed, and removed ones, live only in memory: the evaluation (saved and synced) holds only what was kept, and only the tries used survive a reload. While it records, a red **Recording** tag with the time is pinned in the app bar on every screen (tap it to get back to Stop), so scrolling to the notes never hides it from the team. Every privacy line agrees with the script: the audio leaves the phone only to be transcribed and is deleted once it is (offline it waits on the phone for at most 15 minutes, then is deleted unheard). A recording that finishes offline waits on the phone ("Your notes from this table wait on this phone"; a reload discards them) and is discarded with a toast if the phone isn't back online within 15 minutes of leaving the table. In the previews no audio goes anywhere: the transcript is a labelled sample ("Demo transcript. Your audio wasn't sent anywhere"). Memos (only the judge's voice) are offered after leaving a table that declined, opted out, used up its tries or failed (on the walk, while waiting, or after the last table), never at it, never when Table talk is off for the event, and never when the microphone is blocked or unsupported; a memo gets one retry, and a memo about a past table stays available in that project's page in My notes for the same 15 minutes. Submit and Skip never wait for Table talk; Skip discards what was heard; time-up stops it and keeps the highlights.

    **One policy answer, three locks.** `GavIL.talk.eligibility({eventEnabled, projectOptedOut, judgeSupported, mode})` is the only answer to "may this judge ask the team here?": the event switch (admin), the team's opt-out (a memo, the judge's own voice, ignores it) and this browser. Only an explicit `true` lets the event switch or the browser through. The screen asks it for the card (`TableTalk` `eligibility`: no Ask button, the reason and the next step instead), the parent asks again in the tap that says yes, before an engine starts, and before Resume, so a lock that arrived a moment ago still stops the microphone request. A lock that lands later is never silent: with the consent sheet open it closes the sheet, moves the card back to Ask and record (`onCancelConsent`) and says "… Nothing was recorded." once in the card's live region, so the sheet cannot open by itself when the lock lifts; during a recording or a pause the parent lets go of the microphone at once and says it, a warn toast "Recording stopped. … Nothing was kept." and a warn buzz, because the judge may be looking at the team and not the phone. In the previews the policy is one row (`GavIL.mockServer.policy()`) that the admin console writes and the judge app reads; Table talk is off for an event until counsel signs off, so the judge side fails closed: with no row (or an unreadable one) it is off, exactly what a fresh admin console shows. The one exception is the presenter's walkthrough (`?demo=1` on the judge app), which stands for an event where counsel has already said yes: with no row it has Table talk on and ParkPal opted out, and its States menu writes the row as soon as it changes either. A recording whose microphone track is muted (a call, another app) pauses with "Audio was interrupted" and needs a tap to resume. Highlights that do not come back in 30 s (`talk-processing-timeout`) turn the skeleton into "taking longer than expected" with **Record again** and **Continue without highlights**. The live region says so once and focus moves to the message. Recorded time is capped (`rec-cap`, 8:00) by a wall-clock timer that does not wait for the analyser, and audio held offline is checked against the wall clock on reconnect and when the page comes back, not only by a timer.

**Preview-only pieces.** `GavIL.mockServer` stands in for adonix's *judge* routes (arrive, sync, the Table talk policy). It is demo data and nothing else: four made-up checks (A12 · K7, A17 · M3, A20 · T9, A22 · R4), a made-up QR token (`demo-<code>`) and no secret, key, digest or alphabet, and it has no route that lists or issues a placard, so nothing in the judge bundle can reveal a check before arrival (a real app's placards are issued by adonix to STAFF and ADMIN only; the console previews carry their own mock list). `?demo=1` turns on the presenter's strip (Open email link, Time up, Go offline, States, Restart) and `?walk=0`, which opens each table already checked; the States menu may also switch Table talk to the device's real microphone (the recording is dropped at Stop and the labelled sample transcript stands in). Without `?demo=1` there is no strip and the prototype is the real flow; the strip's controls are 48px like everything else. `?gvtest=1` is the test suite's hook (QR codes and placards carry their payload in a data attribute); production markup never has it.

## Admin rules

Who can do what. Encode this one matrix everywhere: tab state, row state, button state.

| Area | Staff | Admin |
|---|---|---|
| Overview, Judges, Assignments, Deliberations, Prizes & paths, Reimbursements | Read | Read + edit |
| Flagged (anti-cheat) | Read | Clear or escalate |
| Admissions: unassigned applicant | Score, status, notes | Everything + assign |
| Admissions: assigned to me | Score, status, notes | Everything + assign |
| Admissions: assigned to someone else | Read, with a lock and the owner's name | Everything + reassign |
| Settings (add or remove staff/admins) | Read | Read + edit |
| Settings → Judging: Freeze new sign-ins, Table talk, AI summaries, teams opted out of recording | Read (switches locked) | Read + edit (AI summaries asks for an acknowledgement that records who and when) |
| Floor tasks: Restore an absent team / check a team in | Yes | Yes |
| Floor tasks: Confirm absent, close other tasks | Read | Yes |
| Print placards | Print | Print |
| Judges → Void votes (unusually fast judging) | Read | Void, with a required reason in the audit log |

- A tab a role can only read shows a lock (`Tabs` `locked`). It still opens; edit controls inside are disabled with a `lockedReason`.
- Overview leads with work, not stats: the unjudged-projects alert, the flag queue and floor tasks (a table held after 2 absent reports) come first; then **Rooms right now** (a presence map per room: team here, judge walking, judge at the table, held, absent · held, team not here; and the judges, with walking over 4 min and idle over 3 min called out) with **Print placards**; then `CoverageMeter`, then `StatTile`s.
- Placards: `PlacardSheet` prints one `TablePlacard` per page (US Letter, 0.1in short of the printable height so no blank page follows): the room band, the code, its check and the QR of the TABLE (`/t/A12?k=<signed token>`), never the project. Each placard prints its `label` ("A12 · K7") and puts its full 43-character `k` in the QR, exactly what adonix's placards route returns (the preview's mock list makes both; `GavIL.mockServer.demoToken(code)` is a sample 43-character token with no secret behind it). The project's name slot is blank unless the sheet is told to print names (an explicit option, with a note that a moved team then needs a reprint). In the preview the console carries its own mock list (made-up checks, `demo-<code>` tokens); a real console reads it from adonix. The console is given who is signed in and the server's clock by its host (`window.__GAVIL_ADMIN_CONTEXT = { user: { email, name }, serverNow() }`); the audit log and the AI acknowledgement record exactly that, and without it the preview falls back to a mock identity and says so in the header. The Table talk switch and the opted-out teams are written to the one policy row the judge app reads.
- Any project no judge has seen is named in a `danger` alert and served first.
- A project with a hold flag (late commits, a path mismatch, a team over 4) or an escalation never enters the judging queue until an admin chooses **Clear, send to judging** on its `FlagCard`; a review-only flag (a repo older than the event, an unregistered member who may be a typo) is listed and never blocks judging (**Clear flag**). The Flagged tab groups them: held out of judging, escalated with the organizers, review only. The held cells on the presence map, the "held for review" tile and those groups are one fact (a table is held while its project has a hold or escalated flag), so they change together.
- Signing in is a networked step. Offline, the sign-in screen says so in an `OfflineBanner` strip and the send, code and desk-QR buttons wait; a send or a code check that fails says so (`role=alert`), keeps what was typed and spends no try.
- A reimbursement decision (Approve, Deny, Undo) is written to the audit log with who and when; the Deny confirm promises nothing else.
- Destructive actions (Escalate, Revoke, Deny, Remove, Void votes, Confirm absent) confirm inline, on paper, right under the row or card (`InlineConfirm`). Leaving an applicant with unsaved edits asks first. A team confirmed absent stays in the floor tasks as **Marked absent** with **Restore to judging**, so the promise in the confirm is kept. Staff can Restore but not Confirm absent: the console shows them the lock ("Confirm absent: admins only") where the button would be.
- The presence map never relies on colour: each state also has a shape (dashed ring walking there, solid fill at the table, a corner notch held, a struck-through number absent, a dotted ring not here yet), each cell's title names the table, project and state, and each room's grid tells screen readers every table that isn't simply "team here".
- Admission status is one choice (`ChoiceChips`), disabled but readable when the role can't edit it; the reason shows once.
- Below 600px, tables stack into labelled cards (`gv-table--stack`) so names stay on one line and row actions stay on screen; the console never scrolls sideways at 390px.

## Breakpoints (the one exception to "tokens only")

CSS cannot read a custom property inside `@media`, so viewport breakpoints are literals in the stylesheets. Their single source is the `breakpoints` list in `tokens.json` (340px, 420px, 440px, 520px, 560px, 600px, 860px and 900px, each with a usage note); the suite fails on a `@media (max-width: Npx)` in `bundle.css` or a screen that is not listed there, and on a listed width nothing uses. Container queries (`@container`) size components by their own width and stay in em or px beside the rule they belong to. Everything else is a token.

## Scripts

- `python3 scripts/generate_component_docs.py` regenerates every component README, preview and `index.d.ts` (edit the generator, not its output); `--check` writes nothing and exits 1 if a file differs from a fresh regeneration. `build_previews.py` runs `--check`, so a hand-edited README, preview or `index.d.ts` fails the build.
- `node scripts/run_tests.mjs` runs `tests/` headless in both themes (about 550 checks, about 7 minutes each), waits for the page title (`PASS n/n` or `FAIL x/n`, which stays "Gav-IL tests" until the last check), prints every failing row and exits 1 on a failure or a 10-minute timeout. `--theme=night` runs one theme, `--only="Judge prototype · skip"` runs one suite (the tests before it still run, silently, because the suites are one flow), `--throttle=6` slows the CPU like a phone.
- `python3 scripts/build_previews.py` compiles `tokens.json` and wraps every preview for both themes into `.preview/`; it fails if the bundle's consent copy has drifted from `web/lib/talk/policy.ts`.
- `python3 scripts/make_brand_derived.py` writes `assets/brand/derived/` (the phone-sized WebP copies of the brand art and `geometry.json`) from the PNG and SVG originals next to it; `--check` also fails over the 300 KB budget. Nothing is redrawn or recolored, only cropped, resampled and re-encoded.
- `python3 scripts/sync_policy.py` writes the AI-on consent script and team notice in `components/bundle.js` (the two strings between `@policy:begin` and `@policy:end`) and `components/policy.snapshot.json` from `web/lib/talk/policy.ts`; `--check` only reports drift. The suite compares the bundle with the snapshot. Not yet in `policy.ts`, so preview-only here: the AI-off variant, the per-processor lists in the consent sheet and the retention line.
- There is no digest generator any more. The judge bundle used to carry salted digests of every placard check (regenerated by a script from web's dev seed), which let a judge's browser recover a check before arriving; they are gone, so there is nothing to keep in sync with web's dev seed.

Inline SVG line icons on a 24 grid with 2px round strokes in `currentColor`, via `Icon`. **Not used, and why.** `bg-desert-path` is a 1:3.9 portrait strip: behind the 7330 × 900 storyboard it would be blown up 4.8× into a stripe, and behind the Cover (960 × 340) the canyon painting already does the job; the judge screens' ground is the canyon, as the Figma Registration screens have it. `frieze-horses-*` are mid-fi mock references (their X boxes are placeholders). `neon-sign-arrow` is the marquee's post: the marquee alone is the sign on both screens, and the post would need a canvas taller than the Cover's 340px.

**Text on the painting (the scrim rule).** The painting is busy, so the `scrim` token (plum at 66% at Sunset, navy at 65% at Night) lies over it and white text is at least 4.5:1 even on its lightest pixel (5.7:1 at Sunset, 6.1:1 at Night). Content never sits on the painting: it sits on paper cards. Only a title, an eyebrow or a lede may, and an eyebrow is a plum plaque (`gv-step`, `TableProgress tone="plaque"`) so gold text does not depend on what is painted behind it. The suite measures the real pixels, never the flat colour under them: it draws the painting the way the page does (cover-fit, scrim over it), measures each text against the lightest and the darkest 3% of the pixels behind it (a text must clear both) and fails below 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text). Text on a plank or the signpost is judged more strictly, on the pixels under the letters themselves: the plank's 3-slice art is mapped back from the glyph box to the art, the `plank-scrim` or `sign-scrim` is laid over those pixels (the glow at half strength, the value of its blur at the box edge), and the text must hold 4.5:1 at the lightest and the darkest **1%** of them and 3:1 on the single worst pixel.

**Under the lettering on art (the plank-scrim and sign-scrim).** The plank and signpost art have thin highlights and dark grain that a letter can land on: cream on the gold highlights drops to 2.1:1, ink-sign on the darkest tan to 3.7:1. A veil under the lettering fixes the pixels, never the art file. `plank-scrim` (wood-deep at 40%) is a pseudo-element above the border-image and under the label of every wood button and the inline sign: full strength between the end caps, fading to nothing across each cap so no edge shows; its fade is drawn in `wood-deep`, never `currentColor`, so hiding the text never hides it. `sign-scrim` (paper at 22%, blurred) is a glow inside each lettering box of the hero signpost. The Cover's name sign and its Table talk tag are planks too and carry the same veil (a `::after`, since the tag's `::before` is its recording dot): without it the tagline's 14px Rye landed on the gold grain at 3.1:1 at the first percentile and 2.4:1 at the worst pixel; with it 5.4:1 and 4.4:1, the eyebrow 6.5:1 and 5.0:1, the tag 6.6:1 and 5.2:1. The suite measures the Cover in both themes (the plank text, the signpost lettering, the band, the placard) like any screen. Measured on real screenshots (text hidden against text shown, only pixels the glyph covers 85% or more): wood buttons and the inline sign hold 4.8:1 at the worst pixel and 5.7:1 at the first percentile; the signpost's lettering holds 5.5:1 at the worst pixel (6.2:1 at the first percentile).

**Art that does not arrive.** Every other picture is decoration, and decoration that fails never shows the browser's broken-image box. The image reports it (`data-art="failed"`) and the stylesheet takes it out of the layout: a **garland** or a **prop** leaves, an `EmptyState` swaps its prop for its icon medallion, the sign-in **seal** steps aside, the **lockup** becomes its name in plain body type ("HackIllinois", in the cream of the wood under it, so the header never shows clipped alt text; the lockup is Figma art and is never typeset in code, so the fallback does not imitate it), and the **stars** become plain dots in the same three states (ink on paper, gold on the plaque) beside the same count in words, never a mix of stars and broken boxes. A screen reached after the connection drops would otherwise ask for its lazy images only when the Wi-Fi is gone, so once the first project is up the phone fetches the small art the later screens need (`GavIL.prefetchArt()`: the stars, the wagon and the props, about 90 KB) while it is idle. The suite fails the images in place (a token pointed at a missing file) and checks the dots, the text lockup and the prefetch.

**The hero signpost's own states.** The picture is the one image a judge needs, over a loud room's Wi-Fi, to read their table. The sign says where it is in `data-art`: `ready` (decoded; ink on the signs), `loading` (the lettering is hidden for `dur-delay` so a fast load never flashes, then it shows), `failed` (a load error or no art token). In `loading` and `failed` the five lettering boxes become flat wood plates (the wood tokens, no picture, the chamfer, cream lettering with the carved edge, same boxes and rotation), the broken-image box is hidden but keeps its room, and the floor plan stays a 48px control: dark ink on the painted ground is never what a judge sees. `artState` on `Wayfinding` sets it from outside for previews and tests.

**The fit rule (the hero signpost).** What is written on a sign never leaves it. The label takes two lines, then an ellipsis. The noun and the room or table value are one line set at `min(type size, sign width / (characters x sign-glyph))` (container units: the box's own width; the noun never below `sign-fit-min`), ending in an ellipsis at the floor; a value past `sign-fit-chars` (12) drops to `title-sm` on two lines, then an ellipsis, like the project name on the last board. Characters are counted in JS (`--n`), the text is whole in the DOM, and the box clips it (`overflow: hidden`), so a wide fallback font can never spill over the next sign or the post.

**Images.** Every `<img>` has `width` and `height` (the art's own, so nothing shifts), decorative art has `alt=""` and `aria-hidden`, a logo has alt text ("HackIllinois"), anything below the fold is `loading="lazy"`, and the largest image on a judge screen is under 300 KB (the suite adds up the bytes a judge's phone really downloads).
