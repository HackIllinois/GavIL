0. Summary

Question Answer
What are we building? A phone-first app for judges and a desktop admin console. It runs Round 1 (in-person pairwise judging plus prize nominations), Round 2 (deliberations per prize), anti-cheat review, and the admin side of Admissions and Reimbursements.
How will it run? Next.js 15 (App Router) + React 19 + TypeScript, deployed to Cloudflare via OpenNext, the same stack as HackIllinois/site and admin-site. The code goes in the empty HackIllinois/GavIL repo.
Where does data live? In adonix (Express + MongoDB). We add a judging service next to its existing judge service and call it through a client generated from adonix's OpenAPI spec, as admin-site already does.
Auth Judges: passwordless. A one-tap sign-in link or 6-digit code by email, or a QR code at the check-in desk. This reuses adonix's existing sponsor email-code flow, with a new JUDGE role. Staff and admins: adonix's existing Google login, limited to @hackillinois.org, with STAFF/ADMIN roles. One jwt cookie, 24 h expiry.
Algorithm Gavel-style pairwise comparison, adapted from the Figma mock's gavel.ts. The queue runs per room, with a coverage floor of 2 views per project, a conflict list, and a hold on flagged projects.
Look The HackIllinois 2027 brand, pushed toward rodeo / old west: wanted-poster paper cards, saloon wood bars, signpost wayfinding, rubber-stamp statuses, sheriff-star completion, and rosette rankings. Sources: the 2027 Figma Design System, Registration, Website and Theme Moodboard files.

1. Why: what went wrong in 2026

From the Notion judging pages and the 2026 retro: - Everything was manual. Spreadsheets drove room and table assignments, judge routing and tallies. - Judges got lost moving between rooms, and some teams were never judged. - The terms confused everyone. Participants and judges mixed up path, track, prize, company prize and MLH prize. - Sponsors used different rubrics, and corporate and volunteer judges need different orientation. - Anti-cheat was ad hoc: late commits, unregistered members, General teams in Pro.

Goals for 2027 1. Every project is seen by at least 2 judges, and the admin console can show that live. 2. A judge never has to ask "where do I go next?". The app always shows a room and table. 3. Judging is fair and low-bias: pairwise comparisons, and no 1–10 scores in judging. 4. Prize winners come from nominations plus a structured deliberation, not ad-hoc opt-ins. 5. Flagged projects are held out until an admin decides, with the evidence on screen. 6. It works on a phone, one-handed, in a loud room, on bad Wi-Fi.

Non-goals: replacing Devpost; running sponsor-run judging (sponsors may judge their own tracks, and we only record the result); public results pages.

2. Terms (fixed, used everywhere in UI and code)

Term Meaning Examples
Path The division a team competes in Pro, General (Beginner is a member level, not a path)
Track A company-sponsored challenge Stripe, Modal, Caterpillar, John Deere
Prize What a project can win. Three kinds HackIllinois prizes (Best UI/UX, Most Creative, Best Social Impact, Most Useless, Most Popular), company prizes, MLH prizes (no limit on opt-ins)
Nominate A judge marks a project as a standout for a HackIllinois prize Most projects get none
Compare A judge picks the stronger of two projects they have seen No ties
Score Admissions only (0–5 applicant score). Judges never "rate" or "score" projects —

Roles: Judge (volunteer or sponsor), Staff (HackIllinois organizer), Admin (organizer with edit rights).

The Figma mock mixes these terms up. For example, its track field holds Pro/General, and a "Paths" tab is headed "Pro Track". The code uses path for Pro/General and tracks[] for sponsor tracks.

3. How judging works

Before          Check-in (QR at desk) → judge sees path, room, Round 2 prize
Round 1         Same room all round. Loop: walk to table → ~5 min → submit notes + nominations
(in person)     → from the 2nd project on: compare with an earlier project → next table
                Ends at a fixed time; "time is up" lets the judge finish the current project.
Between         Top N most-nominated projects per HackIllinois prize go to Round 2.
Round 2         Each prize's judges meet in a deliberation room, watch Devpost demos,
(deliberation)  each ranks the finalists; combined ranking picks 1st/2nd/3rd.
After           Admins review results + any open flags, then publish to Devpost.

Capacity math, from 2026: 37 judges, 226 projects. A floor of 2 views per project means 452 visits. If each visit takes about 5 minutes plus 1.5 minutes of walking, 37 judges complete about 13.8 visits each in 90 minutes, or about 510 visits. That is about 2.3 views per project. The floor is reachable only if the queue serves unseen projects first and judges never leave their room. The Round 1 window must be at least 85 minutes, or we need more judges. This is an open question for Experience/Outreach.

5. Admin console (laptop first, works on a phone)

Tabs, in order of work:

Tab Purpose
Overview Unjudged-projects alert, flag queue, room coverage, judges active, comparisons, time left
Flagged Anti-cheat queue with evidence. Admin: Clear (send to judging) or Escalate
Judges Who checked in, their path, views so far; send or resend sign-in links
Assignments Judge → room (one room per judge for Round 1)
Deliberations Per-prize combined ranking
Prizes & paths Prize list by kind, eligible paths, deliberation room, nominee counts

The mock ordered its tabs alphabetically (Settings first). We order them by how often staff need them.

Permissions (one matrix, enforced the same way in tab state, row state, button state, and on the server):

Area Staff Admin
Overview, Judges, Assignments, Deliberations, Prizes & paths, Reimbursements Read Read + edit
Flagged Read Clear or escalate
Admissions: unassigned or assigned to me Score, status, notes Everything + assign
Admissions: assigned to someone else Read (lock + owner's name) Everything + reassign
Settings Read Edit

Destructive actions need a confirm with the item's name in it: remove staff, escalate, mass email, deny reimbursement. In the mock, mass email sent with no confirm.

6. Architecture

 Judge phone ─┐                         ┌──────────── adonix (Express + MongoDB) ─────────────┐
 Admin laptop ┼─► GavIL (Next.js 15 on  │ auth: Google (staff), email code/link (judges), JWT │
              │   Cloudflare/OpenNext)  │ judge (existing): judge profiles                    │
              │   • pages + UI          ├─► judging (new): queue, evaluations, votes, skips,  │
              │   • middleware: role    │   flags, coverage, rounds, round-2 rankings         │
              │     gate from JWT       │ admission, registration, … (existing)               │
              │   • generated API client│ Devpost + GitHub sync job (new, anti-cheat)         │
              └─  (openapi-ts)          └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  • Why not a separate database? adonix already owns users, roles, registrations, admissions and sponsors, and anti-cheat needs registration data. A second database would mean syncing two sources of truth.
  • Why Next.js and not Vite? It is the same deploy target and tooling as site and admin-site. Middleware gates routes by role before anything renders. The mock's Vite export ports over as plain React components.
  • Live updates: the judge app polls GET /judging/next when a screen opens and after each action. The admin overview polls every 10 s. No websockets are needed for v1.
  • Offline: writes go into an IndexedDB outbox, each with an idempotency key, and are retried on reconnect. The server rejects duplicates.
  • Undo: a vote is stored with committedAt = now + 8 s. Undo before then deletes it, and the algorithm only reads committed votes.

7. Auth

Who How they sign in adonix
Judge (volunteer or sponsor) Staff add judges in Admin → Judges. A judge enters their email, gets a one-tap link plus a 6-digit code (15-minute expiry, single use), or scans the QR at the check-in desk. The QR is a short-lived signed link New POST /auth/judge/verify/?email= and POST /auth/judge/login/ {email, code}, built on the existing /auth/sponsor/verify + /auth/sponsor/login code flow (AuthCode model). New GET /auth/judge/magic/?token=. New role Role.JUDGE
Staff / Admin "Sign in with Google" → GET /auth/login/google/?redirect=… (existing) Existing STAFF / ADMIN roles; domain limited to @hackillinois.org
  • Session: adonix sets the jwt cookie (24 h, the existing default), and POST /auth/refresh/ renews it. GavIL middleware reads the role to route users, and adonix still checks the role on every API call.
  • Why passwordless for judges: they need to be judging within seconds, many are one-night sponsor guests, and we never want to reset their passwords. Emailed codes already work in adonix for sponsors.
  • Abuse limits:
  • verify always returns success, so no one can use it to check which emails have accounts.
  • Codes and links are single use, and sending is rate-limited per email and per IP.
  • The QR at the desk rotates.
  • A judge's JWT carries only JUDGE and works only for the current event.
  • Sign-out: at the bottom of My notes (with a confirm; the outbox is sent first). Admins can revoke a judge from Admin → Judges.

9. Queue and algorithm

Choosing the next project for a judge (server side, per request): 1. Candidates are projects in the judge's room and path that are not held by a flag, not on the judge's conflict list, and not already seen or skipped by this judge. 2. Coverage first: prefer projects with the fewest views, so everything reaches 2 views before anything gets a 3rd. A project another judge is currently visiting counts as half a view. 3. Break ties by nearest table to the judge's last table (fewer steps), then at random. 4. "Can't find the table" puts the project back at the end of that judge's queue and pings staff with the room and table. 5. No candidates left → Round 1 complete for that judge.

Comparison (from the second project on). The mock's gavel.ts keeps a Gaussian quality belief (μ, σ²) for each project, updated after each vote by a Thurstone/probit Laplace step (σ² floor 0.05). It picks the earlier project whose predicted outcome is most uncertain, with ε = 0.25 exploration. We keep that math, with these changes: - It runs on the server, with state per project shared by all judges. In the mock it ran in each browser and was never saved. - The opponent is chosen from this judge's seen projects, because the judge must remember both. - The rng is seedable and the code has unit tests (ported in web/lib/gavel.ts). - Ranking: order by μ within each path, and show σ² as confidence in the admin console. - Follow-up: move to full Crowd-BT, which adds per-judge reliability (Gavel's α/β), once we have 2026-style data to fit it. This is an open question.

Round 2. Finalists are the top N most-nominated projects per prize, with N set per prize in Settings (default 8). Each deliberation judge ranks all finalists, and a Borda count combines the rankings. Admins see the combined ranking and any ties. A tie is broken by discussion in the room and recorded by an admin.

10. Anti-cheat

A sync job pulls submissions from Devpost and repos from GitHub every 10 minutes until Round 1 starts, then flags: - Late commits: commits after the deadline. The flag shows the hash, time and files. - Unregistered member: a Devpost team member with no HackIllinois registration. - Path mismatch: a Pro submission with General members, or the reverse. - Team size: more than 4 members. - Prior project: the repo was created before the hackathon, or a known previous Devpost project.

A flagged project is held out of the queue. An admin clears it ("send to judging") or escalates it, and the decision goes in the audit log. Copy leads with the evidence, not an accusation. The mock only offered "restore"; we add escalate, a recorded reason, and the hold.

11. Design system

design-system/ in the repo is the source of truth (the published artifact mirrors it): - tokens.json defines two themes, Sunset (purple ground) and Night (2027 Registration blue). It also defines the rodeo materials: paper, wood, rope and brass. - The type comes from the 2027 Figma Style Guide: Ultra for display, Oswald for labels and numbers, Manrope for body text, and Rye for sign lettering. - There are 26 components, plus the clickable JudgePrototype, the AdminConsole and the JudgeJourney storyboard. - tests/ has 107 checks covering flows, keyboard use, offline, permissions, and composited contrast in both themes. - The Next.js app syncs tokens and CSS from design-system/ at build time, so there is one source.

Brand assets to export from Figma (not redrawn): the "H" mark, the longhorn logo, the painted canyon backgrounds, the wooden button textures, and the characters (Characters file) for empty states and completion.

14. Open questions

  1. Round 1 length and judge count (§3 math). At least 85 minutes, or more judges? (Experience)
  2. Where admin lives: inside GavIL (this design system), or as routes in admin-site (Material UI)? Recommendation: GavIL. One brand, and the permission matrix stays in one codebase.
  3. Sponsor-run judging: which sponsors judge their own tracks, and do they use Gav-IL? (Outreach)
  4. Prize limits: is there a cap on how many prizes a team can be considered for? MLH prizes can't be limited.
  5. Hardware: a separate Mechathon run by the sponsor (Notion suggestion), or a track?
  6. Crowd-BT judge reliability: worth it for v1?
  7. Beginner: an unofficial path in Notion. Show it only as a member level?
  8. Room-Setup repo (empty): fold seating plans into adonix RoomPlan?
